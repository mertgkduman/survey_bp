# Feature Research: Policy-Based BP for QLDPC Decoding

**Domain:** Research software, systematic mapping, and reproducible experiments for BP-family quantum LDPC decoders  
**Researched:** 2026-09-21 (America/New_York)  
**Confidence:** MEDIUM overall. The research-plan provider was `websearch`; `classify-confidence --provider websearch --verified` returns MEDIUM. Source-established mechanisms below were checked against primary papers and, where found, original repositories. Project requirements come from [PROJECT.md](../PROJECT.md); they are decisions, not empirical literature findings.  
**Status:** Roadmap scoping research, **not** a completed systematic mapping study or decoder benchmark.

## What the initial journal milestone contains

All three decoding regimes, the mapping study, registry, reference comparisons, documentation, and paper remain in the *first journal milestone*. “First slice” below means implementation order, not a reduced publication scope: (1) CSS code-capacity with separate X/Z decoding and bit-flip noise, (2) correlated quaternary code-capacity, (3) circuit-level decoding. A policy composition is eligible only where its mathematical kernel and observation/noise semantics are defined. This boundary is a project design requirement, not a claim that every named algorithm has been shown to work in all three regimes.

## Feature landscape

### Scientific table stakes

| Feature | Why required | Complexity | Evidence / acceptance idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSS code-capacity input and outcome model | The first stage needs separate decoding of X errors from Z checks and Z errors from X checks, plus bit-flip noise. | MEDIUM | Validate commuting `H_X H_Z^T=0`; retain logical operators; test syndrome validity separately from exact and degenerate recovery. [BPGD, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2) and [Roffe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07016) support the setting. **MEDIUM** |
| Canonical binary BP engine with policy boundaries | The project’s central contribution is one execution engine per compatible kernel family, not wrappers around unrelated decoders. | HIGH | A configuration records the selected update, schedule, state, intervention, stopping, candidate, and optional postprocessing policies; reject undefined combinations with a reason. **Project requirement; implementation proposal** |
| BP update rules | Sum-product, min-sum, normalized min-sum, and offset min-sum are explicit user scope. | MEDIUM | Verify each rule against its defining equations and small hand-computable graphs; distinguish syndrome-based conventions and scale/offset parameterization. Sum-product and min-sum are used in [BPGD](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2) and [ldpc](https://github.com/quantumgizmos/ldpc); normalized min-sum appears in [Maan et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70556-3). Offset min-sum is **project scope; quantum-specific source/semantics to pin**. **MEDIUM/LOW by rule** |
| Message schedules | Flooding, layered, serial, residual, randomized, and cluster-sequential are user scope; update order can change both behavior and work. | HIGH | Record node/edge order, horizontal versus vertical convention, random seed, layer partition, and update count; compare matched schedules on the same graph. [Maan et al., Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70556-3) defines flooding, serial, layered, and randomized variants; [Huang & Ueng](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-01-16-1967/) addresses residual scheduling; [Moradi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20130) defines a *learned* cluster-sequential schedule, which must not be silently generalized to any arbitrary clustering. **MEDIUM** |
| State and memory policy | Damping, mixing, momentum, iteration memory, and disordered memory are user scope. | HIGH | Specify which messages or beliefs retain history, initialization, update equation, precision, and reset behavior. [Gökduman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08177v2) defines a damping equation; [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11523) proposes Momentum-BP for surface codes; [Relay-BP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01779) and [author repository](https://github.com/trmue/relay) support disordered memory. “Mixing” and a generic “iteration memory” need source-specific definitions before implementation. **MEDIUM/LOW by variant** |
| Interventions and candidate lifecycle | Decimation, freezing, syndrome flipping, sign flipping, branching, restarting, relaying, and ensembling are all requested. | HIGH | Each policy defines trigger, mutated state, continuation, branch/ensemble budget, and stopping; ledger records every attempted candidate and its validity. Primary anchors: [BPGD](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2), [Poulin–Chung](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.1241), [BP-SF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00254), [branch-assisted sign flipping](https://doi.org/10.1109/TQE.2023.3279379), [BeamSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07057v2), [Restart Belief](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13281), and [Relay-BP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01779). These are *different source algorithms*, not evidence that the interventions commute. **MEDIUM** |
| Optional nonconvergence processing | OSD/LSD and source-defined SI/OTF must be independently switchable, with BP-only results observable. | HIGH | For each run record whether BP converged before fallback, fallback type/order/work, and final outcome; compare BP-only versus BP+fallback on paired error samples. [BP-OSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07016), [BP-LSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18655), [SI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06125), [OTF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01440). **MEDIUM** |
| Outcome and effort ledger | Syndrome satisfaction alone does not establish logical success; iterations alone do not capture branching or postprocessing. | HIGH | Every shot retains code/logical identity, noise draw, seed, policy and backend versions, events, candidate list, termination reason, syndrome validity, exact/degenerate recovery, logical failure, post-selection, message/branch/linear-algebra work, and runtime. This is a **project evaluation contract**, motivated by distinct candidate outcomes in [BeamSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07057v2) and distinct exact versus degenerate decoding in [BPGD](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2). |
| Paper-to-experiment registry and manifests | A named decoder without paper settings and executable evidence is not a reproduction. | HIGH | Each supported paper has a machine-readable manifest with exact policy/parameters, code and logical-operator hashes, noise, p grid, seed policy, budget/stopping, versions, expected outputs, statistical/numerical tolerance, original paper/repo links, and discrepancies. An executable entry resolves the manifest to outputs and figures. **Project requirement; design proposal** |
| Two evaluation views | Faithful published settings and controlled shared settings answer different questions. | HIGH | Reproduction uses source settings and declared curve tolerances; controlled runs pair codes, noise, error samples, and explicit work budgets, reporting logical error, convergence, iterations, work, and runtime separately. **Project requirement; design proposal** |
| Correlated quaternary code-capacity | The journal study must later decode X/Z jointly under an explicit Pauli distribution rather than infer correlated behavior from independent binary runs. | HIGH | Joint four-state variable and both stabilizer types; test Y errors and correlated priors; compare to separately decoded binary baseline under the *same* channel. [BPGD, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2) provides a quaternary decimation example. **MEDIUM** |
| Circuit-level decoding | The paper must include noisy syndrome-extraction circuits and the corresponding observations, fault variables, logical observables, and decoder semantics. | HIGH | Pin circuit, detector error model or equivalent graph, detector/observable mapping, rounds, and per-shot faults; test known single-fault propagation before curves. [BeamSearch paper/repo](https://github.com/ionq-publications/BeamSearchDecoder), [Relay-BP repo](https://github.com/trmue/relay), [OTF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01440), and [GARI-NMS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70556-3) show distinct circuit-level paths. **MEDIUM** |
| Detailed documentation and journal manuscript | Users must interpret policies, evidence, discrepancies, and statistical conclusions; the paper covers all three regimes. | HIGH | Publish equations, compatibility matrix, original-source inventory, manifest schema, reference integration instructions, known deviations, experiment scripts and figure provenance. Complete the manuscript only after the three-stage evidence exists. **Project requirement** |

| Feature | Value | Complexity | Evidence status |

| Executable original implementations beside canonical realizations | An external reference preserves the published algorithm as a comparison target even if policy mapping is incomplete. | HIGH | Original repos verified for Relay-BP, BeamSearch, BP-OSD/BP-LSD; BPGD and impulse original repos **not verified** in this scoping pass. **MEDIUM/LOW** |
| Literature map joined to experiment registry | Enables a reader to move from claim → paper/version → mechanism → code/noise/settings → run → figure. | HIGH | The mapping workflow is a **design proposal** informed by [Petersen et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584915000646); [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) offers reporting guidance, not a substitute for the protocol. **MEDIUM** |
| Provenance-preserving event ledger and controlled comparisons | Allows analysis of convergence, computational work, runtime tails, candidate behavior, and logical correctness under paired samples. | HIGH | **Project design requirement**; no superiority is assumed. |

### Anti-features: explicit scope boundaries

| Temptation | Why it would mislead | Preferred boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A common facade over unrelated decoders as the sole “unification” | It hides update and state semantics, so policy effects cannot be isolated. | Original decoders remain reference executions; compatible algorithms are expressed in a canonical engine. |
| Universal policy combination | Binary LLR, quaternary Pauli, and circuit detector graphs have different domains and conditioning. | A compatibility matrix with source-defined parameter mappings and explicit unsupported cases. |
| Always-on OSD/LSD, or judging only final postprocessed output | It obscures whether the BP intervention helped BP converge. | Independent fallback switch and pre/post-fallback outcomes. |
| Treating syndrome validity or exact error recovery as logical success | Different errors can share a syndrome; degenerate correction can differ from the physical error. | Separate syndrome, exact, stabilizer-equivalent, and logical labels. [BPGD definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10950v2). |
| Calling this seed inventory a completed systematic map | Search, screening, extraction, and completeness checks have not yet occurred. | Protocol, search log, dual-screening/adjudication or documented alternative, extraction schema, and flow counts before publication. |
| Deferring quaternary or circuit-level work beyond the first paper | Conflicts with the approved journal scope. | Sequential implementation, shared provenance from the first stage, all three regimes in the initial journal result. |

**Acronym caution.** In the cited papers, SI means *stabilizer inactivation* and BP+OTF means *ordered Tanner forest*. The BP-LSD paper separately uses the phrase *on-the-fly elimination*; its mechanism is different even if another source shortens that phrase to OTF. The user’s exact intended SI/OTF variants and candidate-voting semantics were not specified in PROJECT.md; map a concrete source and mathematical action before coding them. A generic majority vote over error vectors need not preserve the measured syndrome, so voting requires a source-defined object (candidate, logical class, or other), eligibility rule, and final syndrome validation. **This last constraint is a mathematical design inference, not a published performance result.**

## Dependency and acceptance map

```text
Source inventory + mapping protocol
    → versioned paper/specimen record → reproduction manifest → executable registry entry
CSS code + logicals + bit-flip sampler → binary BP kernel → policy hooks + ledger
    → BPGD / impulse / Relay-BP / BeamSearch canonical variants
    → optional OSD/LSD/SI/OTF + reference comparisons
    → faithful reproduction and controlled paired comparisons
Binary policy/evidence contracts → correlated quaternary kernel + joint Pauli sampler
Quaternary/code-capacity evidence → circuit, fault/observation/observable semantics
All three evidence stages + completed systematic map → journal manuscript
```

The source inventory and schema should start **alongside** binary implementation. A source paper can be in the mapping corpus before it has an executable reproduction entry; “supported paper” begins only when its manifest and executable entry are complete. This avoids presenting a bibliography as a reproducibility registry.

Candidate-processing order matters. First record raw BP hard decisions and posterior information. Next record source-defined branch/freeze/decimation effects. Next validate candidate syndromes and select from eligible candidates using the named rule (e.g., minimum weight in [impulse Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18240v1); fixed beam width and score in [BeamSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07057v2)). Only then run optional OSD/LSD-style fallback on nonconvergence under an explicitly recorded trigger. “List selection” and “voting” remain separate policies with no default semantics inferred from the other algorithms.

For each named policy realization, a meaningful acceptance test is: (1) published toy example or fixed small input follows source-defined state transitions, (2) reference and canonical executions agree on *defined invariants* (input syndrome, candidate validity, stopping labels) without demanding identical trajectories, (3) paper-specific curves meet prespecified statistical tolerance or carry a documented discrepancy, and (4) paired BP-only/fallback-on runs expose whether gains arise before or after postprocessing. These are **proposed acceptance criteria**, not outcomes already demonstrated.

## Roadmap prioritization within the first journal milestone

| Sequence | Deliverable | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 foundation | Mapping protocol and seed corpus, code/noise/logical identity, binary CSS kernel, update/schedule and ledger contracts | Reproducible small-code BP baseline; captured search/screening rules; manifest schema and first executable entry |
| P1 BP modifications | Binary source-faithful BPGD, Relay-BP, impulse, BeamSearch plus intervening schedule/memory policies; reference execution where original code exists | Source-to-policy mapping, unit-level state checks, BP-only ablations, recorded budgets and candidate outcomes |
| P1 optional candidate processing | Independent OSD/LSD fallbacks and source-resolved SI/OTF/list/vote rules | BP-only versus fallback-on paired trials and explicit nonconvergence triggers; no silent fallback |
| P1 correlated code capacity | Quaternary kernel and joint Pauli model; applicable policies ported by explicit compatibility argument | Known Y/correlation cases and matched binary/quaternary comparisons under the same channel |
| P1 circuit level | Circuit/noise/observation specification, reference integration, applicable canonical policies | Single-fault checks, detector/observable mapping, paper-specific curves with confidence intervals |
| P1 publication | Completed systematic map, controlled comparison suite, documentation, archived manifests/outputs, journal manuscript | Search and selection flow, reproducible tables/figures, discrepancies and statistical limits, results from **all three** regimes |

“P1” denotes required publication scope, not simultaneous development. This sequence protects the source-defined BP mechanisms as the scientific priority while allowing optional fallback implementations to provide fair baselines. It does not predict which decoder performs best.

## Mapping-study protocol requirements

The seed table above is **not** an exhaustive literature census. Before claiming a systematic mapping study, define research questions and inclusion/exclusion criteria; search databases with saved query strings and dates; deduplicate records; screen titles/abstracts and full texts with decisions and reasons; snowball citations under a recorded rule; extract a versioned taxonomy of kernel, update, schedule, memory, intervention, candidate processing, code, noise, outcome metric, implementation, and evidence type; reconcile ambiguous labels; and publish counts, data dictionary, and an update cutoff. [Petersen et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584915000646) motivate classification and counting for maps. [PRISMA 2020](https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020) supplies checklist/flow reporting tools; whether every PRISMA item fits this computing map requires protocol-level judgment. Both are **MEDIUM** confidence via verified primary/guideline sources.

## Open source-identity and semantics work

- Locate an author-confirmed BPGD implementation and an impulse-decoding implementation, if public. Search absence is not proof of nonexistence. Do not call third-party QBelief an “original BPGD repo” without author confirmation.
- Check whether the user intended SI specifically as stabilizer inactivation and OTF specifically as ordered Tanner forest, and whether “voting” means over candidate corrections, logical predictions, or ensemble members. Record any alternate interpretation in the mapping schema.
- Pin precise definitions and primary QLDPC references for offset min-sum, mixing, generic iteration memory, list selection, and the requested freezing/restarting variants. A name alone is insufficient to define a canonical policy.
- Determine a legal integration strategy for the [BeamSearch repo’s noncommercial ShareAlike license](https://github.com/ionq-publications/BeamSearchDecoder/blob/main/LICENSE) before copying code into an open platform; executable reference access and independent reimplementation are distinct.
- Specify circuit-level graph construction and work units before comparing circuit results. [BeamSearch](https://github.com/ionq-publications/BeamSearchDecoder), [Relay-BP](https://github.com/trmue/relay), and [GARI-NMS](https://github.com/astra-decoders/gari-nms) demonstrate different representations, so a shared detector-error-model input alone does not prove policy equivalence.
