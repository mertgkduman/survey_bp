# Two-thirds presentation p-axis diagnostic

## Result

A common `p_local = 2 p_slide / 3` shift does not reconcile the supplied presentation curves with the local paired pilots. At double-Gross slide `p=0.08`, the local shifted run at physical `p=0.053333...` gave vanilla BP4 SP `43/1000` versus slide `0.0033`, tuned BP4 SP `7/1000` versus `0.00077`, and tuned binary OMS `0/1000` versus `0.03153`. The exact one-sided 95% upper bound for the binary zero is `1 - 0.05^(1/1000) = 0.002991...`.

For B1 slide `p=0.10`, the shifted local run at `p=0.066666...` gave vanilla BP4 `22/100`, tuned BP4 `8/100`, and binary `0/100`, versus slide `0.0054`, `0.00032`, and `0.04571`. The 100-shot zero is less conclusive by itself, but both BP4 discrepancies are large. At double-Gross slide `p=0.12`, the existing local `p=0.08` BP4 results `15/100` and `7/100` are closer to slide `0.12195` and `0.11455`, but binary `3/100` is far below slide `0.4898`. The opposite shift `p_local = 3 p_slide / 2` at double-Gross slide `p=0.08` fits worse across all three arms.

The factor `2/3` belongs to the binary X or Z marginal probability under depolarizing noise: `P(X component=1) = P(X)+P(Y) = 2p/3`. It does not, by itself, relabel the physical depolarizing probability on a plot shared by BP4 and binary decoders. The pilot tests only a common x-axis reinterpretation; it does not isolate possible differences in BP4 recurrence, scheduling, code construction, or decoder-specific priors. The slide rates are treated as exact in the diagnostic binomial p-values because their raw counts are unavailable.
